2026 Outlook:
Turning AI investment into results.

Organizations are investing heavily in AI, but technology alone will not improve performance. They also need the skills, processes and customer understanding to put it to work.

Jille Kuipers
Published on 5 February 2026, 12:00 • Hong Kong
Save (PDF)

In 2026, organizations face two pressures at once: technology is changing quickly, while people still value stability and dependable services. For businesses, the challenge is to use new technology to address real problems, rather than investing in it for its own sake.

Investment alone is not enough. Organizations also need the skills, processes and customer understanding to put new tools to work. Having data and technology does not automatically mean a company knows how to use them well.

I. Turning AI investment into business value

Some analysts believe AI could help economies grow faster than the long-term trend. AI may also help researchers generate, test and improve ideas in science and materials. Whether this leads to lasting gains will depend on how effectively organizations apply it.

Global AI spending is projected to reach between $5 trillion and $8 trillion through 2030. To earn a reasonable return, the largest technology companies may need to generate about $1.6 trillion more in annual revenue. The focus is shifting from the equipment that supports AI—such as chips and data centres—to products and services that create measurable value for businesses and customers.

Fig 1.1: AI investment and the revenue needed to support it

Growth also depends on physical resources. By 2030, AI data centres are projected to use 15% to 20% of total US electricity demand. The availability of power could affect how quickly AI infrastructure expands. Organizations will need to use both technology and people effectively.

II. Why reliability and trust matter

People are experiencing rapid change in technology and society. Accenture describes the resulting fatigue as “narrative whiplash.” Its research found that 71% of people surveyed expected 2026 to be a better year, while nearly half were preparing for a possible recession.

Fig 1.2: The UX Ripple Effect — Cross-Industry Expectation Cascades

The value of dependable service

When people face constant change, clear information and dependable service can build trust. Research cited in this report found that 75% of consumers want facts rather than misleading or low-quality AI-generated content.

People also compare services across industries. A fast and simple experience with a retailer can raise expectations for healthcare, insurance and public services. Organizations are not judged only against direct competitors; customers also compare them with the best digital services they use.

III. Priorities for organizations in 2026

Three practical priorities stand out: serve people at different life stages, test ideas carefully and use AI to support people at work.

1. Serve people at different life stages

The global longevity market is projected to reach $8 trillion by 2030. People over 40 have significant cultural and financial influence, and many continue to work, start businesses and take on new roles later in life. Organizations should consider people’s needs at different life stages, rather than relying only on age groups.

2. Test ideas in a disciplined way

Innovation is a process, not a single flash of inspiration. Organizations need room to test ideas, but they also need a clear way to decide what to develop and deliver. Keep experiments focused, learn from the results and connect successful tests to practical plans.

Fig 2.2: Where AI-related revenue may be created

3. Use AI to support people at work

AI can help people with both physical and knowledge-based work. For example, it may guide a healthcare professional through a task or help a manufacturing worker use complex equipment. The aim should be to support people’s skills, not assume that replacing them is the goal.

Summary: adapt while staying grounded

New technology can create opportunities, but established ways of working can make change difficult. Organizations can build on what they already do well while adapting to new tools and needs. The central question for your 2030 strategy is: are you planning around the technology, or around the people you serve?

A practical next step

Organizations need to connect their plans, people and customer needs. An innovation review can help identify what is working and where to focus next.
